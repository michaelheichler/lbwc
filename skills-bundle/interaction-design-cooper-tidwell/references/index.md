# Reference index: patterns and principles by chapter

Route by pattern name or concept, not just by chapter. Each entry gives the
file, a one-line summary, the concepts it covers, and the named patterns or
principles it defines. Find the pattern or principle you need, then open that
one file. Load one to three files, not the whole catalog.

Two books, two jobs. About Face decides what to build and how it should behave.
Designing Interfaces gives the concrete named pattern to build it with.

## About Face 4th Edition (Cooper, Reimann, Cronin, Noessel)

### Chapter 1. A Design Process for Digital Products
File: `about-face-ch01-a-design-process-for-digital-products.md`
Digital products fail without a systematic process, and Goal-Directed Design
fixes that by grounding every decision in user goals rather than tasks,
features, or implementation convenience.
Concepts: goal-directed design, implementation model vs mental model vs
represented model, goals versus tasks and activities, capability viability
desirability, personas, user research and empathy, conflict of interest
between design and development, design process phases.
Named: Goal-Directed Design (six phases: Research, Modeling, Requirements
Definition, Framework Definition, Refinement, Support), Implementation model /
mental model / represented model, Goals versus tasks and activities (contrasted
with Norman's Activity-Centered Design), Capability, viability, desirability
triad (Larry Keeley, Doblin Group).

### Chapter 2. Understanding the Problem: Design Research
File: `about-face-ch02-understanding-the-problem-design-research.md`
Design decisions come from qualitative research into user goals and behavior,
gathered through a sequence of stakeholder, SME, and ethnographic user
interviews built on a provisional persona hypothesis.
Concepts: qualitative vs quantitative research, stakeholder interviews, SME
interviews, ethnographic interviews, contextual inquiry, persona hypothesis,
behavioral variables, open vs closed-ended questions, leading questions,
usability testing timing, focus groups, card sorting, task analysis.
Named: Ethnographic interview, Contextual inquiry (Beyer and Holtzblatt) and
its four principles (Context, Partnership, Interpretation, Focus), About Face's
modifications to contextual inquiry, Persona hypothesis, Man-on-the-street
observation, Open-ended vs closed-ended questioning, Leading question, Coding
(ethnographic note organization).

### Chapter 3. Modeling Users: Personas and Goals
File: `about-face-ch03-modeling-users-personas-and-goals.md`
Turn field research into personas built from clustered behavior patterns and
driven by explicit experience, end, and life goals, so the team aims at one
specific human instead of an elastic, self-referential, or edge-case user.
Concepts: personas, user goals, experience end and life goals, elastic user,
self-referential design, edge cases, persona construction, behavioral
variables, persona types (primary, secondary, supplemental, customer, served,
negative), provisional personas, market segments vs personas, workflow and
artifact and physical models.
Named: The elastic user, Self-referential design, Implementation-model
products, Edge cases, Don't make the user feel stupid, Archetypes vs
stereotypes, User role / role model, Participatory design, Primary persona,
Secondary persona, Supplemental persona, Customer persona.

Named: Define what the product will do before you design how it will do it,

### Chapter 5. Designing the Product: Framework and Refinement

Concepts: Design Framework, interaction visual industrial and service framework,

Named: Design Framework, Pretend the product is human, Rectangles phase, Key

### Chapter 6. Creative Teamwork
File: `about-face-ch06-creative-teamwork.md`
Good design comes from small, clearly-roled core teams that pair generative and
evaluative thinking, plus deliberate practices for coordinating with the wider

Concepts: generator and synthesizer roles, core team vs extended team, team
sizing, cross-discipline collaboration, interaction vs visual vs industrial

### Chapter 7. A Basis for Good Product Behavior

Concepts: design values, design principles, design patterns, ethics in

### Chapter 8. Digital Etiquette
File: `about-face-ch08-digital-etiquette.md`
People unconsciously treat software as a social actor, so design it to behave
like a considerate, smart, socially fluent colleague rather than a rude,
forgetful, or intrusive machine.
Concepts: considerate vs polite software, computer does the work while the
person does the thinking, memory of user actions, task coherence, decision-set
reduction, preference thresholds, fudgeability, social vs market norms,
Dunbar's number, idle cycles and speculative computation, graceful failure and
undo, griefing and moderation tooling.
Named: The considerate product characteristics checklist, Fudgeability, Task
coherence, Decision-set reduction, Preference thresholds, Mostly right most of
the time, Social norms vs market norms, Dunbar's number.

### Chapter 9. Platform and Posture
File: `about-face-ch09-platform-and-posture.md`
Choose platform and posture (sovereign, transient, or daemonic, plus web,
mobile, and embedded variants) before detailing screens, because posture sets
how much attention the product can claim and how bold or quiet it should be.
Concepts: platform vs posture, sovereign, transient, and daemonic posture,
informational vs transactional vs web-application sites, satellite and
standalone posture, ten-foot interface, findability, kiosk and automotive
design, intermediate-user optimization.
Named: Sovereign posture, Transient posture, Daemonic posture, Informational
website posture, Transactional website posture, Web application posture,
Satellite posture, Standalone posture, Ten-foot interface posture, Findability
(Peter Morville).

### Chapter 10. Optimizing for Intermediates
File: `about-face-ch10-optimizing-for-intermediates.md`
Design primarily for the perpetual intermediate, the bulk of users who are
neither first-time beginners nor master experts, by inflecting the interface
around real usage frequency, dislocation, and risk.
Concepts: perpetual intermediates, skill distribution bell curve, commensurate
effort, progressive disclosure, inflecting the interface, frequency of use,
degree of dislocation, degree of risk exposure, beginner onboarding vs
reference help, expert vs beginner optimization.
Named: Commensurate effort, Progressive disclosure, Inflection of the interface
(frequency of use, degree of dislocation, degree of risk exposure), Perpetual
intermediates (vs Constantine's improving intermediates).

### Chapter 11. Orchestration and Flow
File: `about-face-ch11-orchestration-and-flow.md`
Design the whole interaction so the product stays out of the user's way and
preserves flow, judging every element against the current goal with fourteen
harmonious-interaction strategies.
Concepts: flow, orchestration, modeless feedback, mental models, latency
perception bands, command vs configuration, ejector-seat levers, animation and
transitions, avoid blank slates, probable vs possible.
Named: Flow, Orchestration (harmonious organization), No matter how cool your
interface is less of it would be better, Follow users' mental models, Less is
more, Let users direct rather than discuss, Provide choices rather than ask
questions, Keep necessary tools close at hand, Provide modeless feedback,
Design for the probable but anticipate the possible, Contextualize information,
Reflect object and application status.

### Chapter 12. Reducing Work and Eliminating Excise
File: `about-face-ch12-reducing-work-and-eliminating-excise.md`
Every interaction costs users cognitive, memory, visual, or physical work.
Strip out any work that does not advance the goal (excise), the leading cause
of product dissatisfaction.
Concepts: excise vs goal-directed tasks, navigational excise, skeuomorphic
excise, modal excise, stylistic excise, signposts and overviews, mapping
(Norman), monocline grouping vs hierarchies, flow interruption, posture-
dependent excise tolerance.
Named: Excise (goal-directed vs excise tasks), Eliminate excise wherever
possible, Navigational excise, Navigational trauma, Skeuomorphic excise, Modal
excise, Don't stop the proceedings with idiocy, Don't make users ask
permission, Allow input wherever you have output, Stylistic excise, Signposts,
Overviews.

### Chapter 13. Metaphors, Idioms, and Affordances
File: `about-face-ch13-metaphors-idioms-and-affordances.md`
Design idiomatic, learn-once interfaces instead of physical-world metaphors,
and signal every control's manipulability with the right affordance and
pliancy hint for the platform.
Concepts: metaphoric vs idiomatic vs implementation-centric interfaces, manual
affordance, global metaphor pitfall, direct manipulation (Shneiderman),
pliancy, static, dynamic, cursor, and pliant-response hinting, interaction
vocabulary pyramid, org-chart-centric interface.
Named: Manual affordance, Global metaphor, Idiomatic design, Interaction
vocabulary pyramid (primitives, compounds, idioms), Direct manipulation
(Shneiderman, 1974), Pliancy, Static hinting, Dynamic hinting, Pliant response
hinting, Cursor hinting, Org-chart-centric interface.

### Chapter 14. Rethinking Data Entry, Storage, and Retrieval
File: `about-face-ch14-rethinking-data-entry-storage-and-retrieval.md`
Data entry, storage, and retrieval should follow the user's mental model of a
single forgiving document and a smart search index, not the database's need for
clean, pre-typed, pre-located records.
Concepts: data integrity vs data immunity, modeless error feedback,
fudgeability, audit trail, unified file model, autosave, Save Changes dialog
probability error, positional vs identity vs attribute-based retrieval, digital
soup, folksonomy, constrained natural-language output.
Named: Data integrity vs data immunity, Audit don't edit, Fudgeability, Unified
file model, Positional, identity, and attribute-based (associative) retrieval,
Digital soup, Folksonomy (Thomas Vander Wal), Constrained natural-language
output.
